Classes on Hold for ‘Viksit Bharat’; Principal Busy in Photo-Op with Modi Cut-Out

At Kalindi College, New Delhi, students were told attendance would be marked for participation in the BJP government's 'Viksit Bharat' initiative — events scheduled during class hours, as the college principal posed for photographs at a Modi cut-out. A ground report on the line between government scheme and institutional compulsion.

By Mouli Sharma
14 February 2024 · 4 min read

‘Attendance’ to be marked for participation in ‘Viksit Bharat’ events taking place during class hours.

14th February 2024, New Delhi: The current phase of the government’s ‘Viksit Bharat’ ( Developed India ) initiative, “ a nationwide campaign to raise awareness through outreach activities to achieve saturation of schemes of Govt. of India across the country covering all [Local Bodies] ,” is taking place in educational institutes, including higher education institutes like the University of Delhi.

From hours-long photo ops, workshops, surveys & quizzes (take the Viksit Bharat quiz on the Hamara Sankalp Viksit Bharat website to get a certificate from PM Modi himself!) all culminating in the Viksit Bharat Pledge, a wide range of ‘events’ under the sun are taking place in various colleges of Delhi University under the aegis of internal quality assessment committees, media societies, or department management. Some colleges show no participation at all, implying that none of these are regulation procedures, & are being informally implemented. At one Kalindi College, the campaign has taken over day-hours with many of the activities directly clashing with & being given priority over lectures, & overworked faculty canceling several of the rest. But here’s the catch; attendance is still being marked.

With DU’s allotment of 5% of student’s final grade ending up at null for attendance below 67%, & arbitrary cancellation of classes around the year by professors being a regular & consequence-less matter, attending or not attending a 3-hour event can mean the difference between having ‘attended’ or not ‘attended’ 3 lectures at a time on paper, none of which were in fact conducted. This is unfair to students who travel long distances daily Kalindi, one of DU’s many sans-hostel colleges, leading to a large number of students ignoring the ‘mandatory’ attendance of these workshops to stay home & study.

“I haven’t been attending the events. I’m not interested. How many Youth India schemes have come before this? Have they ever improved anything?” says a student of Kalindi College’s Journalism Department, which is the most significantly impacted. One of the more recent departments to suffer mass dismissals & replacements after interviews for permanent faculty, KC’s small team of 8 professors is involved not only with its heavily stacked schedule of Viksit Bharat events within the department (being the media representatives of the college) but also with the press & coverage of all others.

With DU’s infamous academic calendar already lagging behind in the 2023-24 session as always (the even semester began a staggering 16 days after the commencement date marked on the university’s initial calendar, delaying examinations till the last week of May), ongoing faculty interviews, & the university being in the transition period of the NEP, students and faculty are already stretched thin in an attempt to complete syllabi, & these generic, unnecessary, school-like activities - being carried out under the guise of improving the reach of government schemes to secluded communities - are taking precious time away from near graduate students.

But instances like these aren’t limited to the University of Delhi. The participation statistics of the Viksit Bharat Mission quoted on their official website continue to grow, & the reality of this ‘participation’ as far as being conducted in higher education institutes is that is forced, involuntary, & consistently being carried out at the threat of academic sabotage. At the capital’s Netaji Subhash Technological University, students were handed out ‘Viksit Bharat’ promotional tasks as ‘assignments,’ failing to complete which would mean wasting their previous semester.

“We had to send in some idea related to viksit bharat. The college people wouldn’t return our previous semester marksheets otherwise.” says a third-year B.Tech student at NSUT’s east campus.

What business do the Universities partaking in these activities have to do so? What business does the government have making them?

What part of saturating government schemes is taking a selfie with a cardboard cutout of Narendra Modi & uploading it to a gov.in website?
