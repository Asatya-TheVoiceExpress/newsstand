'Section 144' at IMT Manesar: Police Violence Against Protesting Maruti Workers Intensifies Ahead of Tripartite Meet, Violates Court Order

Haryana police deployed in force on 29th-30th January 2025 to disrupt the Maruti Suzuki Asthayi Mazdoor Sangh's planned mass demonstration at IMT Manesar — invoking Section 144 of the now-obsolete IPC despite a court order permitting the gathering. A photo essay and press release from the scene.

By Mouli Sharma
30 January 2025 · 12 min read

Members of the Maruti Suzuki Asthayi Mazdoor Sangh (MSAMS), were arrested & forcibly dispersed from their protest site at IMT Manesar ahead of their mass demonstration against the company management, for which they had sought & recieved the local Court's permission. Despite the favourable court order, officers of the Manesar, Gurgaon & Pataudi police were deployed in large numbers to disrupt the workers' gathering, claiming an imposition of Section 144 of the now obsolete IPC. Police declined to comment on the situation, & even threatened to detain media persons present on site. Following is the official press release of the MSAMS.

January 30, 2025: IMT Manesar remained the scene of widespread chaos throughout 29th and 30th January as the Haryana police lay siege over Manesar tehsil, the site for a massive demonstration and march of non-permanent workers of Maruti Suzuki challenging the system of hire-and-fire of workers and demanding permanent jobs, equal pay, and other facilities for temporary workers at par with permanent workers. The unmeasured unleashing of repression on peaceful protesters indicates how threatened the Maruti Suzuki management feels by any questions being raised about its hire and fire labour policies, the main source of its massive profits. It also revealed the hand and glove coordination of the company and the local administration.

Seeking to preempt the gathering, the administration imposed Section 144 around Manesar Tehsil on the evening of January 29. The following morning, thousands of workers pouring into the area for the protest were packed into buses and taken to the Manesar police lines station or let off at different locations 40-50 km from the protest site. Others were chased through fields with lathis.

The Maruti Suzuki management has now approached the Gurgaon civil court twice, first asking for a permanent injunction against the terminated workers and now an injunction against the temporary workers. In both instances, the court recognized the right of workers to demonstrate peacefully 500m away from the company gate and boundary. However, as soon as the court pronounced its orders on the morning of January 29, the police started uprooting the demonstration site of the workers in front of Manesar tehsil.

Khushiram, a worker terminated in 2012 and legal advisor of the Asthayi Mazdoor Sangh spoke to local media on the night of 29th January, “We have never gone against the law, while the company has flouted all labour laws and destroyed the lives of over 11 lakh workers over the last 13 years. “Not going back without the jobs we deserve”: Maruti temporary workers

Late Morning, 30th January 2025: After the first round of arrests, workers who had travelled from neighbouring states felt disheartened & fearful for their safety as well as that of their detained fellows, but their desire to continue the mobilisation ahead of tomorrow's meeting remained absolute. Image courtesy: Mouli Sharma for TVE

Workers argue that while the company has constantly expanded its sales and profits, the workers who have made this possible have been left out in the cold with no security of employment and labour rights. The company presently employs 83% of its labour force on short-term contracts which state that “Your appointment is purely temporary in nature and has arisen due to fluctuating business and exigencies of work.” Only 5713 permanent workers out of the total workforce of 34,918 enjoy the benefits and high salaries that the company claims to provide its workers. The company has been recruiting workers directly after school in the name of running an ITI and conducting skill development, others are recruited as temporary workers (TW) for 7-month-long contracts, and many others as contract workers for similar tenures ranging from a few months to a couple of years. Workers are promised to be called for another tenure after some time if they maintain full attendance and work without any complaints. However, not more than a handful are called for a second or third term. Thousands of workers are called for the Company trainee (CT) exam which is seen as a doorway to permanency every year, but only 30-40 workers are finally made permanent across the three plants. This process keeps workers engaged in an on-and-off way through the best years of their lives, i.e. from 18 to 25 after which there is no hope for being recruited again. The experience letter and training certificate provided by the company hold no value in the job market as the workers are placed directly in production and spend their entire time on only one or two stations, gaining no definite skills.

Non-Permanent workers and Terminated permanent workers unite to form a formidable force

A movement for establishing their union initiated by Maruti Suzuki Manesar workers in 2011 is remembered as one of the most militant and important trade union struggles in the country in the last few decades. The year-long agitation succeeded in forming an independent union of workers on March 1, 2012 but was soon sought to be crushed by the management with an outbreak of violence on July 18 of the same year. The company had terminated 1800 contract workers and 546 permanent workers without an enquiry on the pretext of the violence and 147 workers were jailed out of which 31 were convicted and 117 were acquitted after five years in jail while 13, constituting the union body, were sentenced to life imprisonment. Workers terminated in 2012 have remained organised since, and have played an active role in running the criminal and labour cases of all the terminated workers, as well as ensuring support to jailed workers and their families. They have been demonstrating at the site since September 18, 2024 demanding reinstatement of all the workers acquitted by the court and the state SIT. These workers have provided constant support to the young temporary workers in formulating their demands and organizing themselves. Ravinder Dahiya, a worker who was terminated in 2012 said that they understood the massive scam of temporary employment being run by the company when they sat in IMT Manesar with their own demands and started talking to workers, feeling that this was an issue that has to be raised at all costs. “We are not going to withdraw our agitation till our demands are met. We will not let this company exploit the youth of our country and spit them out into a hopeless future. Our struggle in 2011-12 set an example of the unity between permanent and contract workers. Permanent workers struck work to get contract workers reinstated when the company tried to lock them out. Permanent recruitment of contractual workers was the main ground on the stalemate reached on the first charter of demands submitted by our then newly founded union before the July 18, 2012 incident.”

A fight for our collective futures

The past decade has seen a massive rise in the use of temporary workers in main production processes. While being a cost-cutting measure, such a labour regime also allows the company to maintain a very high work intensity, such that only the very young and able can manage, and which is unsustainable for workers after a few years. It has also allowed the management to scuttle the rights won by unions of the permanent workers, formed after numerous militant struggles. Hundreds of unions were formed in the belt, and the salaries and benefits of permanent workers saw a sharp improvement after numerous historic struggles throughout the 2000s and especially after the 2011 strikes in Maruti Manesar. The Covid lockdown saw many layoffs and shutdowns, marking a setback to the permanent worker’s unions which no longer have the same hold over production, nor the impulse for militant struggle. The brunt of production has been shifted onto young non-unionized temporary workers who till now had found little space to articulate their deep distress.
